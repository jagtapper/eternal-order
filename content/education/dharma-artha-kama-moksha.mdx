A lot of modern talk about Hindu life starts in the middle. Identity, temple politics, what someone said on a panel. The older books start earlier. They ask what a life is *for*.

The answer they keep giving is fourfold. *Purushartha*: the aims of a person. Dharma, artha, kama, moksha. Not four personality types. Four goods that have to share one life without eating each other.

## Dharma

*Dharma* is the one everyone uses and almost no one defines. In this set it is the restraint and the duty that keep the other three from becoming a mess. How you earn, how you love, even how you seek release, has to answer to something larger than mood.

It is also older than "ethics" as a school subject. Behind dharma sits *rta*, the sense that the world has a grain. You can run with it or against it. Law is what you write when that grain is no longer felt.

If you only remember one thing: dharma is not a party adjective. It is the question, what can I do here without lying?

## Artha

*Artha* is means. Livelihood, property, skill, a surplus that can feed a house and, when needed, a people. Sanatan thought did not treat wealth as dirt. It treated *unbound* desire as disorder.

This is why self-reliance belongs on this site. A person who cannot stand on their own feet will call that condition a philosophy. Artha under dharma is capacity. Artha without dharma is just appetite with better furniture.

## Kama

*Kama* is desire: love, beauty, pleasure, children, the ordinary hunger to be close to someone. The tradition put it on the list on purpose. A civilization that pretends people do not want is not serious.

The warning is simple. Kama is legitimate. It is not sovereign. When it becomes the only aim, the house thins out. When it is forbidden as if it were a crime, the house lies. The old map tries to hold both facts at once.

## Moksha

*Moksha* is release. Freedom from the loop. Different schools describe the method differently. The function in this fourfold is easier: it keeps the other three from pretending they are the last word.

Without moksha, dharma hardens into policing. Artha becomes hoarding. Kama becomes a speech about freedom that is mostly appetite. The fourth aim is the window in the room.

## How to use the map

You do not pick one and throw the others away, unless you have taken a path that asks that of you. For most lives the work is proportion. Earn without becoming only your earning. Love without burning the house down. Keep duty without turning it into cruelty. Remember that even a well-run life is not the whole story.

If someone is shouting about Hindu identity and cannot place these four, they are shouting from a later layer. Go back a step. The rest of this series assumes you have.
