Ganesh Chaturthi is not only *modaks*, clay, and sweets. Those are the door. The house behind the door is a claim about how a psyche is supposed to grow.

## Two directions of desire

A familiar Western story of the psyche runs like this: the son grows toward the mother and against the father. Desire climbs. Conflict is the engine of maturity.

Jung, reading Eastern maps in *The Psychology of Kundalini Yoga*, noticed the opposite weather. In one story-system, desire is trained to rise from below. In another, the climb is not allowed to run the house. The Sanatan map does not deny desire. It relocates it.

That is not a clinical diagnosis of anyone's family. It is a civilizational mental model: what a people trains the mind to do with appetite.

## The door, the boy, the cut

Parvati posts a son at the door. Shiva comes home. The boy blocks the father. The father takes the head — the human head that would learn to think with the body first.

What returns is not a softer appetite. It is a head rebuilt for *buddhi*: discrimination, the cut that says this, not that. In the fourfold map of the inner instrument (*citta*, *manas*, *buddhi*, *ahamkara*), *buddhi* is the judge. Without it, memory is weather and desire never lands.

The elephant trunk, in this reading, is not a costume. It is the reward pathway remade on the skull. Desire is still there. It is channeled. Ganesha is not consumed by reward. He routes it. The obstacle-remover begins with a severed ego and is kept as the figure of wisdom.

Atomic line: there is no Oedipus in the Sanatan psyche. *Buddhi* after the cut.

## What the festival is asking

On Ganesh Chaturthi you are not only watching a clay form go into water. You are watching the Eternal Order relocate reward to wisdom. The inversion is the point.

Sanatan Dharma, as this journal uses the phrase, is philosophies of lived order — not a weekend religion you join. The festival is pedagogy. It teaches a people how not to let appetite write the constitution of the mind.
