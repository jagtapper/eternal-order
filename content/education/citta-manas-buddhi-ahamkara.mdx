You are close. The four names are right. The frame is slightly off.

Vedic, yogic, and Vedanta teaching does not usually say there are four *types* of memory. It says the inner instrument, the *antahkarana*, has four functions. Together they explain how a person takes in a world, keeps it, judges it, and claims it as mine.

The common English spellings:

- *Citta* or *chitta* (चित्त)
- *Manas* (मनस्)
- *Buddhi* (बुद्धि)
- *Ahamkara* (अहंकार). *Ahankar* is the same word, shortened the way Hindi often shortens it.

Some schools list only manas, buddhi, and ahamkara, and treat citta as the field they work in. The fourfold list is the usual classroom map. Both are trying to describe one inner workshop, not four separate souls.

## Citta

*Citta* is mind-stuff. The store of impressions (*samskara*), the sediment of what you have seen, done, feared, and loved. When this journal talks about civilizational memory, this is the closest inner word for it. A people has a citta too: habits, stories, hurts, and skills that sit below the week&apos;s argument.

Yoga spends a lot of time here. The *Yoga Sutra* speaks of stilling the movements of citta. That is not anti-thought. It is a claim that the store can be so stirred you cannot see what is in it.

## Manas

*Manas* is the mover. It takes what the senses bring, holds two options at once, wants, doubts, and flits. It is useful. It is also why you can reread the same line three times and still not decide.

Manas does not finish anything. It presents. If you treat every flicker of manas as a command, you will call that freedom and live inside weather.

## Buddhi

*Buddhi* is discernment. The faculty that cuts: this, not that. Duty rather than mood. A true memory rather than a flattering one.

Without buddhi, citta is a warehouse with no clerk and manas is a market that never closes. This is why the four aims need an inner map. Dharma is not only a public word. It is buddhi doing its job.

## Ahamkara

*Ahamkara* is the I-maker. The sense that a memory, a skill, a slight, a people is *mine*. You need some of this to get out of bed and keep a vow. You drown in it when every impression has to serve the story of who you are.

Ahankar, the shorter spelling, is the same faculty. It is not a moral insult by default. It becomes one when it swallows the other three.

## How they work as one

A sound reaches you. Manas turns toward it. Citta offers a matching impression: last time this meant danger, or home, or a slight. Buddhi judges whether that match is true now. Ahamkara stamps it: this happened to *me*, or to *us*.

That is not four memories. That is one act of knowing, taken apart so you can see where it usually breaks.

A civilization can fail in the same four places. It can lose the store (citta). It can live only in agitation (manas). It can stop discriminating (buddhi). It can make every fact about identity (ahamkara). The outer maps on this site, the four aims and the four functions, do not work if the inner instrument is only weather.

Start here when someone says "memory" as if it were one drawer. It is a workshop.
