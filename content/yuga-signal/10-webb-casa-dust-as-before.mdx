On Nov 23, 2026, NASA Science reported that Webb's mid-infrared instrument (MIRI) resolved dust in individual, solar-system-sized clumps in the Cassiopeia A supernova remnant. Spitzer data picked three targets. One reverse-shocked clump was mostly silica dust. Another was dominated by carbon dust. The "Green Monster", made of material the star shed before it exploded, was rich in complex magnesium silicates.

NASA says the data support a "shell merger": the oxygen- and carbon-burning shells inside the star mixed, possibly just hours before core collapse. Merger signatures appear across much of the remnant. NASA notes that remnants like this "will eventually seed a new generation of stars and planets."

## The parallel

> sūryācandramasau dhātā yathāpūrvam akalpayat | divaṃ ca pṛthivīṃ cāntarikṣam atho svaḥ

Rig Veda 10.190.3 (Aghamarṣaṇa sūkta). The Ordainer (Dhātā) shaped the sun and moon "as before" (yathāpūrvam), and heaven, earth, the mid-air and the light. The padapāṭha glosses yathāpūrvam as "as before". Griffith renders it "formed in due order". Sanskrit and padapāṭha confirmed on Wisdomlib; Griffith at Sacred Texts.

## Why it sits here

This is a comparison, not a claim of foresight. The hymn describes the cosmos as re-made "as before", one turn in a repeating order. Webb's result is a measurement: the elements a star built in layers are scattered, condense into dust and, in NASA's words, seed later stars and planets. The ancient texts describe a cycle. The telescope shows one turn of matter's cycle, star → dust → star, in detail. That is corroboration of a cyclical picture, nothing more.

## The Yuga signal

**Capability.** Clump-level dust chemistry across one remnant, in a peer-reviewed letter. The shell merger is "supported", not proven, and its timing ("possibly hours") is model-dependent. The method still has to be applied to other remnants before it becomes a general tool.
