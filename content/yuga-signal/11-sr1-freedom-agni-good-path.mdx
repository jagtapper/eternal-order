On Nov 24, 2026, NASA said it had completed a major mission design review for Space Reactor-1 (SR-1) Freedom at Glenn Research Center in October and locked key elements of the spacecraft. NASA describes it as the first fission-powered spacecraft to travel beyond Earth orbit. It is targeted for launch in late 2028 and will demonstrate nuclear electric propulsion in deep space for the first time.

The locked decisions include a shorter boom between the reactor and the Power and Propulsion Element, improved shielding so the reactor can sit closer to the spacecraft, and larger X-band antennas. Radiator count, reactor output power and reactor run time are still being evaluated. In the mission profile, the reactor activates after leaving Earth orbit, runs for an initial period, then shuts down. Solar arrays power the cruise. The reactor restarts after SkyFall (three Mars helicopters) is delivered to Mars orbit. NASA awarded a $39.3 million contract to Johns Hopkins APL for the heat rejection system.

**This is a design milestone, not a launch.** The reactor itself is being developed by DOE's Idaho National Laboratory. No flight hardware has flown.

## The parallel

> agne naya supathā rāye asmān viśvāni deva vayunāni vidvān

Rig Veda 1.189.1, which also closes the Īśā Upaniṣad (verse 18). "By goodly paths lead us to riches, Agni, God who knowest every sacred duty" (Griffith). Fire is asked to guide a journey, by a good path, because it knows the ways. Sanskrit confirmed on Wisdomlib; Griffith at Sacred Texts; Īśā 18 at Heritage IIT Kanpur.

## Why it sits here

This is a comparison only. The verse treats Agni as the guide of a journey along a good path. SR-1's design lets a controlled fire, fission heat turned into electricity, carry a craft along a planned trajectory. It is switched on only beyond Earth orbit, rested during cruise and restarted at the destination. The rhyme is in the principle: fire is trusted to lead when it is kept in order. Nothing in the hymn describes reactors.
