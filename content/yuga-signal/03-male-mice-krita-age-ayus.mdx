In aged male C57BL/6J mice (19 treated, 20 controls), three months of vanadyl sulfate in drinking water raised median survival to 952 days from 886.5 days. A University of Chicago-led team (Wu, Yang, Li et al.; senior author Jing Chen) reported the result in Nature Aging.

The compound selectively killed senescent cells in lab and mouse models, and aged mice of both sexes showed better endurance and metabolic measures. Lifespan in female mice was not reported. The authors say long-term safety still needs full evaluation and that excess vanadium can be harmful.

**This was a mouse study, not a human trial.**

Disclosure: according to the paper's competing-interests statement, Jing Chen is an inventor on a pending US patent application for vanadyl sulfate compositions for aging-related diseases.

## The parallel

Isha (Īśāvāsya) Upanishad, verse 2.

## Why it sits here

The paper reports more than extra days. The old mice also showed better endurance and metabolic measures, so the added time was more active time. The Isha asks for the same pairing: a long life that is still a working life. It is not a promise that this compound extends human life.

## The Yuga signal

**Life extension.** A treatment started late in life raised median survival in aged male mice by about 7%. This is an animal study with a small sample and male-only lifespan data. Long-term safety has not been evaluated, and there is no human trial.
