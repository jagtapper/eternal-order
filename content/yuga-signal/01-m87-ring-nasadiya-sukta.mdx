A CSIRO team of Samuel Lai, Nithyanandan Thyagarajan, O. Ivy Wong and Foivos Diakogiannis re-imaged the public Event Horizon Telescope data on M87* from 2017, 2018 and 2021. Their method, GenDIReCT, works from closure invariants, quantities that instrument-calibration errors do not disturb, and it does not need a hand-set starting image.

Across epochs and fields of view it recovered a ring about 42.2 ± 1.5 microarcseconds wide, which implies a mass of about 6.5 ± 0.5 billion suns. That largely corroborates the 2019 EHT result and disputes a 2022 reanalysis that found a blob instead of a ring. The authors say the black hole's spin is still unconstrained.

## The parallel

> ā no bhadrāḥ kratavo yantu viśvato 'dabdhāso aparītāsa udbhidaḥ

"May good concepts come to us from every side, un-beguiling, unhindered, and beneficial." Rig Veda 1.89.1, in Pt. Sri Rama Ramanuja Achari's translation (*Sūkta Saṅgraha*).

## Why it sits here

The ring became more trustworthy when a different team, using a different method, could test it on data anyone can download. The hymn asks for exactly that: good thinking, from any direction, unhindered. This is a habit of inquiry rooted in the Vedas, not a claim that the Rig Veda describes black holes.

## The Yuga signal

**Knowledge access.** Open telescope data let a team outside the original analysis re-check a flagship result and help settle a dispute. This is basic science: no material abundance follows from it directly, and the black hole's spin is still unknown.
