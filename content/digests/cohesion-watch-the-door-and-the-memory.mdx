This is a weekly pulse. Not a membership drive. Not a scorecard of who showed up where. A look at what is binding a people, and what is inviting them to split.

Michigan's Hindu Community Relations Council received $300,000 in the state budget, championed by Rep. Tom Kuhn, for safety, emergency prep, and leadership training across more than forty Hindu and Jain temples. That is *artha* in the old sense of the word: means to keep a house standing. Use it. Do not become grant-dependent for identity. The state can help guard the door. It cannot remember who we are.

That memory still has to be taught, funded, and shared across mandirs that do not share a language or a calendar. That part is svadharma. No legislature can do it for you.

## Continuity in brick

In Penn Hills, Pennsylvania, Sri Venkateswara Temple marked fifty years this month. Ground was broken in 1976. A four-day gathering closed the anniversary weekend. It is one of the first purpose-built Hindu temples in North America.

When someone says the community "just arrived," point at the stone. Institutional continuity is civilizational memory you can walk into.

## The split that keeps getting offered

A large Hindu gathering is scheduled for New York later this month. Friends and opponents will both try to turn attendance into a purity test. Do not take it.

The old fragmentation pattern now wears civic clothes: competing events, incompatible stories of who we are, each circle sure the other is the problem. Do not pick a faction. Return to the shared spine. Teach the epics as living guides. Stand in your own duty. Ally without demanding sameness.

Shivaji did not wait for the 96k to become identical. He bound many duties to one Swarajya. Michigan does not need one temple. It needs that same refusal to outsource memory.

A community that cannot name its own dharma will be named by others. That was true on campus last year. It is true this week. The teaching has not changed.
